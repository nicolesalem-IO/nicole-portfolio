Crisp legally and ethically sound wellness policy

1. Purpose Statement

Crisp, a hybrid SaaS organization operating across multiple time zones, is implementing a Wellness Benefits and Incentive Policy to strengthen employee well-being, reduce cognitive and physical strain, and support sustainable engagement across distributed teams. Because Crisp’s work involves periods of high cognitive load and rapid collaboration, employees benefit from structured opportunities to recover energy, establish healthy routines, and maintain psychological stability. The purpose of this policy is to provide equitable access to wellness resources that support physical activity, nutritional health, and stress reduction, regardless of job type or work location.

To promote these goals, Crisp partners with Intermountain Health Care (SelectHealth) to offer employees up to $240 annually in gym membership reimbursement, processed entirely through the insurance provider to protect health-related privacy. Hybrid employees working near the Salt Lake City office will have access to optional group exercise activities during lunch hours, such as walking groups or stretching sessions, and the office will maintain a fully stocked refrigerator with fresh fruits, vegetables, and healthy meal ingredients. Remote employees will receive a $50 monthly wellness stipend to ensure that they have the same ability to purchase healthy food and participate in wellness activities from home. These offerings align with Crisp’s values of inclusion, equity, and psychological safety by ensuring that participation is voluntary, accessible, and never dependent on employees disclosing medical or personal information.

2. Legal and Ethical Compliance

This wellness policy complies with federal nondiscrimination and privacy laws while maintaining clear ethical commitments to fairness and voluntary participation.

2.1 ADA Compliance

All components of the program are designed to be accessible to employees with physical, sensory, or psychological disabilities. Employees may request modifications, such as alternative activities, remote-access options, or adjusted participation methods, without being required to disclose a medical diagnosis. ADA-related accommodations are managed confidentially through Human Resources in accordance with EEOC guidelines (U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission [EEOC], 2020).

2.2 HIPAA Compliance

Crisp does not collect, store, or monitor any protected health information. The gym reimbursement benefit is processed solely through Intermountain Health Care (SelectHealth), meaning employees submit documentation directly to the provider rather than to Crisp. Because the organization does not access biometric, fitness, diagnostic, or medical data, the program remains fully HIPAA-compliant (U.S. Department of Health & Human Services [HHS], 2023).

2.3 GINA Compliance

Crisp does not collect or request genetic information, family medical history, or any data that could be interpreted as genetic indicators. Participation in all wellness activities is voluntary, and employees are never asked to explain reasons for nonparticipation. This structure avoids any risk of violating the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA).

2.4 EEOC Nondiscrimination Requirements

All wellness offerings apply equally to employees regardless of disability, age, race, gender identity, religion, pregnancy status, or caregiving responsibilities. Participation or nonparticipation has no impact on performance evaluations, compensation, advancement, or job assignments. Supervisors are prohibited from requesting personal health details or pressuring employees to participate in wellness activities.

2.5 Ethical Foundations

The program is grounded in ethical commitments to autonomy, fairness, privacy, and inclusion. Employees choose whether to engage in activities without fear of stigma or disadvantage. No biometric tracking, step counting, or performance-based incentives are used. This approach supports psychological safety and aligns with research emphasizing that well-being programs must protect employee dignity and personal agency to be effective (Adamska-Chudzińska & Pawlak, 2025).

3. Sample Policy Language for Employee Handbook: Employee Wellness Benefits & Incentive Program

Crisp supports employee wellness as an essential component of a healthy, productive workplace. The Wellness Benefits and Incentive Program provides voluntary opportunities to promote physical activity, nutritious eating, and social connection across hybrid teams.

Program Offerings

  • Gym Membership Reimbursement: Employees enrolled in Intermountain Health Care insurance may receive up to $240 per year for gym reimbursement. Documentation is submitted directly to the insurance provider. Crisp does not receive health data or fitness information.
  • On-Site Wellness Activities: Hybrid employees may participate in optional lunchtime group wellness activities, including walking groups, stretching sessions, or yoga. Participation is voluntary and not tracked.
  • Healthy Food Access: Crisp maintains a fully stocked office refrigerator with fruits, vegetables, and healthy snack options. Remote employees receive a $50 monthly wellness stipend to support equivalent access to nutritious food.
  • Wellness Education: Optional educational sessions on stress management, nutrition, and emotional well-being are available throughout the year.

Confidentiality and Rights

  • Participation in all wellness offerings is voluntary and will not affect employment status, evaluations, or compensation.
  • Crisp does not collect or store biometric, medical, or genetic information.
  • Employees may request ADA accommodations privately through Human Resources.
  • Employees may opt in or out of activities at any time without explanation.
